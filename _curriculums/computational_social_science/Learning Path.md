# Learning Path — Computational Social Science & AI

## Mission

Computational social science with a cognitive foundation: **Psychology → Sociology**, with **CS/AI as the instrument** and **Philosophy (~15%) as connective tissue**. The goal is to be able to ask a real question about human behaviour, gather or find the data, model it honestly, and argue about what it means in clear prose.

## Stage 1 (Months 0–6): Foundations

**Coding**
- CS50x (Harvard) **or** the Boot.dev Python track.
- pandas: load CSVs, clean, group, and plot.
- Ship one small project per month into `code/` (start with `csv-plotter`).

**Math**
- *OpenIntro Statistics* (free textbook) — chapters 1–7.
- Khan Academy: Statistics & Probability; Algebra II review.

**Reading**
- Mortimer Adler — *How to Read a Book*
- Daniel Kahneman — *Thinking, Fast and Slow*
- Robert Cialdini — *Influence*
- Jonathan Haidt — *The Righteous Mind*
- OpenStax — *Psychology 2e* (free)
- Plato — *Apology* and *Crito*
- Marcus Aurelius — *Meditations*
- George Orwell — *Essays* (start with "Politics and the English Language")

**Philosophy**
- Bertrand Russell — *The Problems of Philosophy*
- *forall x: Calgary* — formal logic (free)
- John Stuart Mill — *Utilitarianism*

**Writing**
- Biweekly 1,000-word argument: position in sentence 1, two reasons, one objection answered.

**People Skills**
- One listening-only conversation per week; log 3 things learned in `06 People.md`.
- Read Dale Carnegie — *How to Win Friends and Influence People*; Charles Duhigg — *Supercommunicators*.

**Stage 1 Benchmarks**
- [ ] (`s1_csv`) Write a Python script from a blank file that loads a CSV and plots something from it.
- [ ] (`s1_stats`) Explain p-values, confidence intervals, and why correlation is not causation.
- [ ] (`s1_read`) Read 150 pages of serious nonfiction in a week and summarize each chapter's argument from memory.
- [ ] (`s1_write`) Write 1,000 words taking a position, with two reasons and one objection answered, in under 3 hours.
- [ ] (`s1_biases`) Name three cognitive biases caught in yourself this month.

## Stage 2 (Months 6–18): Building Capacity

**Coding**
- MIT 6.0001 — Introduction to Computer Science and Programming in Python.
- MIT 6.006 — Introduction to Algorithms.
- One human-data project per month (survey, text, or network data).
- Andrew Ng — Machine Learning Specialization (around month 12).

**Math**
- Calculus I — MIT 18.01 or Khan Academy.
- MIT 18.06 — Linear Algebra (Gilbert Strang).
- Intermediate statistics and regression.

**Reading**
- Steven Pinker — *How the Mind Works*
- Dan Ariely — *Predictably Irrational*
- Primary sources: Milgram (1963), Asch (1951/1956), Tversky & Kahneman (1974) "Judgment under Uncertainty"
- Peter Berger — *Invitation to Sociology*
- Erving Goffman — *The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life*
- Mark Granovetter — "The Strength of Weak Ties" (1973)
- Émile Durkheim — *Suicide*
- Nicholas Christakis & James Fowler — *Connected*
- Duncan Watts — *Everything Is Obvious*

**Philosophy**
- Thomas Kuhn — *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*
- John Stuart Mill — *On Liberty*
- René Descartes — *Meditations on First Philosophy*
- Gilbert Ryle — *The Concept of Mind*
- John Rawls — *A Theory of Justice* (Part I)

**Writing**
- Monthly 2,500-word essay with 3+ sources.
- Weekly 300-word reading response.
- Two project write-ups (methods, results, limits).

**Full Courses**
- Yale Introduction to Psychology (Paul Bloom).
- An Introduction to Sociology course (e.g., Coursera / OpenStax Sociology 3e).
- MIT 18.06 with problem sets.

**People Skills**
- An 8-week improv class.
- Bring up one remembered detail per week.
- One specific compliment per day.
- Read Marshall Rosenberg — *Nonviolent Communication*.

**Stage 2 Benchmarks**
- [ ] (`s2_numpy`) Implement linear regression and a simple neural network from scratch in NumPy.
- [ ] (`s2_math`) Take a derivative, multiply matrices by hand, and explain what eigenvalues are for.
- [ ] (`s2_soc`) Explain Milgram, Asch, and Granovetter and what each implies for the other two.
- [ ] (`s2_write`) Write a 2,500-word essay a harsh reader calls interesting, not just correct.
- [ ] (`s2_courses`) Finish three full university courses including problem sets.
- [ ] (`s2_listen`) Hold a conversation listening for 10 minutes without analyzing.

## Stage 3 (Months 18–36): Depth

**Coding & AI**
- fast.ai — Practical Deep Learning for Coders.
- Stanford CS229 — Machine Learning.
- Stanford CS224n — NLP with Deep Learning.
- NetworkX for social network analysis; Mesa for agent-based models.
- Backpropagation from scratch.

**Math**
- Harvard Stat 110 — Probability (Joe Blitzstein).
- MIT 18.02 — Multivariable Calculus.
- Judea Pearl — *The Book of Why*.
- Scott Cunningham — *Causal Inference: The Mixtape* (free online).

**Reading**
- Matthew Salganik — *Bit by Bit: Social Research in the Digital Age*
- Robert Putnam — *Bowling Alone*
- Shoshana Zuboff — *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism*
- James C. Scott — *Seeing Like a State*
- Douglas Hofstadter — *Gödel, Escher, Bach*
- Current papers: ICWSM, CSCW, PNAS, *Nature Human Behaviour*.

**Philosophy**
- Daniel Dennett — *Consciousness Explained*
- David Chalmers — *The Conscious Mind*
- Andy Clark — *Supersizing the Mind*
- Karl Popper — *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* / *Conjectures and Refutations*
- Stuart Russell — *Human Compatible*
- Shannon Vallor — *The AI Mirror*
- Kant (*Groundwork*) or Aristotle (*Nicomachean Ethics*)

**Writing**
- Quarterly 4,000-word papers.
- Final 6,000–8,000 word original research paper, submitted publicly.

**People Skills**
- Host a regular gathering (dinner, reading group, game night).
- Practice warm disagreement.
- Read Stone, Patton & Heen — *Difficult Conversations*; David Brooks — *How to Know a Person*.

**Stage 3 Benchmarks**
- [ ] (`s3_model`) Train a model on human data (text, survey, network), interpret it, and write it up with limits.
- [ ] (`s3_abm`) Build a working agent-based model and explain what emerged and why.
- [ ] (`s3_causal`) Identify whether a published finding is causal or correlational, and design a study to settle it.
- [ ] (`s3_openq`) Name three open questions in computational social science and the main positions on each.
- [ ] (`s3_paper`) Write 6,000+ words making an original claim with a literature review.
- [ ] (`s3_grad`) Hold your own with a graduate student in psychology, sociology, or ML.
